1. The Nucleus: Mass Defect & Radioactive Decay (L21)

Mass Defect $\Delta m$ & Einstein's Mass–Energy Relation:

$$\Delta m = \left[Z \cdot m_p + (A - Z) \cdot m_n\right] - m_{\text{nuc}} \quad \text{or} \quad \Delta m = \sum m_{\text{react}} - \sum m_{\text{prod}}$$ $$\Delta E = \Delta m \cdot c^2, \qquad \Delta E_{\text{mol}} = \Delta E \cdot N_A, \qquad E_B\text{ per nucleon} = \frac{\Delta E}{A}$$

Radioactive Decay Law (First-Order Kinetics) & Activity $A$:

$$A(t) = -\frac{\text{d}N}{\text{d}t} = \lambda \cdot N(t), \qquad \lambda = \frac{\ln 2}{\tau_{1/2}}, \qquad N_0 = \frac{m_0}{M} \cdot N_A$$ $$N(t) = N_0 \cdot e^{-\lambda t} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \frac{A(t)}{A_0} = \frac{N(t)}{N_0} = e^{-\lambda t} = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{t / \tau_{1/2}}$$

2. The Shell: Photons, Matter Waves & Confined Electrons (L22)

Photon Energy, Bohr–Rydberg Transitions & De Broglie Wavelength:

$$E_{\text{Ph}} = h \cdot \nu = \frac{h \cdot c}{\lambda} = h \cdot c \cdot \tilde{\nu}, \qquad \tilde{\nu} = \frac{1}{\lambda} = \tilde{R}_H \cdot Z^2 \left(\frac{1}{n_f^2} - \frac{1}{n_i^2}\right)$$ $$\lambda_{\text{de Broglie}} = \frac{h}{m \cdot v} = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2 \cdot m_e \cdot e \cdot U}}, \qquad \Delta x \cdot m \cdot \Delta v_x \ge \frac{h}{4\pi}$$

Particle in a 1D, 2D (Square) & 3D Box:

$$E_n\text{ (1D)} = \frac{h^2 \cdot n^2}{8 \cdot m_e \cdot L^2}, \qquad E_{n_1,n_2}\text{ (2D)} = \frac{h^2}{8 \cdot m_e \cdot L^2}(n_1^2 + n_2^2)$$ $$E_{n_x,n_y,n_z}\text{ (3D)} = \frac{h^2}{8 \cdot m_e}\left(\frac{n_x^2}{L_x^2} + \frac{n_y^2}{L_y^2} + \frac{n_z^2}{L_z^2}\right)$$
Fundamental Constants (Lecture & Exam Reference Values):
  • Planck constant: $h = 6.6261 \times 10^{-34}\text{ J s}$; Speed of light: $c = 2.9979 \times 10^8\text{ m s}^{-1}$
  • Elementary charge: $e = 1.6022 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$; Avogadro constant: $N_A = 6.0221 \times 10^{23}\text{ mol}^{-1}$
  • Atomic mass unit: $1\text{ u} = 1.66054 \times 10^{-27}\text{ kg}$; Electron mass: $m_e = 9.1094 \times 10^{-31}\text{ kg}$
  • Nucleon rest masses: $m_p = 1.00728\text{ u}$, $m_n = 1.00866\text{ u}$
  • Rydberg constant for hydrogen: $\tilde{R}_H = 1.09737 \times 10^7\text{ m}^{-1} = 109737\text{ cm}^{-1}$
⚠️ Golden Unit Conversion Rules (Nuclear & Quantum):
  • Electronvolt: $1\text{ eV} = 1.6022 \times 10^{-19}\text{ J}$, $1\text{ MeV} = 10^6\text{ eV} = 1.6022 \times 10^{-13}\text{ J}$
  • Energy equivalent of $1\text{ u}$: $1\text{ u} \cdot c^2 = 1.4924 \times 10^{-10}\text{ J} = 149.24\text{ pJ} = 931.49\text{ MeV}$
  • Activity (Becquerel): $1\text{ Bq} = 1\text{ decay s}^{-1}$ (always convert $\lambda$ to $\text{s}^{-1}$ before computing $A = \lambda \cdot N$ in $\text{Bq}$!)
  • Length scales: $1\text{ nm} = 10^{-9}\text{ m}$, $1\text{ pm} = 10^{-12}\text{ m}$, $1\text{ \AA} = 10^{-10}\text{ m} = 100\text{ pm}$

Guided Example 1 • Ref: L21_The Nucleus.pdf & Q02_Quizzes_Lecture2.pdf (1st Rep, Q2 & Q4)

Deuterium–Tritium Fusion Mass Defect ($^2_1\text{H} + {}^3_1\text{H} \rightarrow {}^4_2\text{He} + {}^1_0\text{n}$) & $^{131}\text{I}$ Fallout Decay Kinetics

Problem Statement:
Part I (Nuclear Fusion, Quiz Q02 1st Rep Q2): In a fusion reactor, deuterium ($^2_1\text{H}$) and tritium ($^3_1\text{H}$) fuse according to $^2_1\text{H} + {}^3_1\text{H} \longrightarrow {}^4_2\text{He} + {}^1_0\text{n}$. The nuclide and particle masses are $m(^2_1\text{H}) = 2.01410\text{ u}$, $m(^3_1\text{H}) = 3.01605\text{ u}$, $m(^4_2\text{He}) = 4.00260\text{ u}$, and $m(^1_0\text{n}) = 1.00866\text{ u}$ ($1\text{ u} = 1.6605 \times 10^{-27}\text{ kg}$, $c = 2.9979 \times 10^8\text{ m s}^{-1}$).
Part II (Radioactive Decay, Quiz Q02 1st Rep Q4): The fission product $^{131}\text{I}$ accumulates in the thyroid gland and decays by $\beta^-$ emission with a half-life of $\tau_{1/2} = 8.00\text{ d}$.
(a) Calculate the mass defect $\Delta m$ (in $\text{u}$) and the energy $\Delta E$ released in a single fusion event (in $\text{J}$ and $\text{MeV}$).
(b) Calculate the molar energy $\Delta E_{\text{mol}}$ released when $1.00\text{ mol}$ of $^2_1\text{H}$ fuses with $1.00\text{ mol}$ of $^3_1\text{H}$ (in $\text{GJ mol}^{-1}$), and compare it to typical chemical combustion ($\sim 10^2\text{–}10^3\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$).
(c) Calculate the decay constant $\lambda$ (in $\text{d}^{-1}$) of $^{131}\text{I}$ and the percentage of initial activity $A(t)/A_0$ remaining after $t = 20.0\text{ d}$.

🎙️ Lecturer Board Moderation (Guided Example 1):

"Emphasize why we keep all 5 decimal places when subtracting nuclear masses: the mass defect $\Delta m = 0.01889\text{ u}$ is less than $0.4\%$ of the total mass, yet multiplying by $c^2 \approx 9 \times 10^{16}\text{ m}^2\text{ s}^{-2}$ turns this tiny mass loss into $1.70\text{ billion kJ mol}^{-1}$ — roughly six orders of magnitude larger than chemical bond energies!"

"In step (c), remind students that as long as $t$ and $\tau_{1/2}$ have the SAME time unit (both in days), $\lambda \cdot t$ is dimensionless and we do not need to convert days to seconds for $A(t)/A_0$ — we only need seconds when calculating absolute activity in Becquerel ($\text{Bq} = \text{s}^{-1}$)."

Step (a) – Mass Defect $\Delta m$ & Single-Event Fusion Energy $\Delta E$:
$$\Delta m = m(^2_1\text{H}) + m(^3_1\text{H}) - \left[m(^4_2\text{He}) + m(^1_0\text{n})\right] = (2.01410 + 3.01605) - (4.00260 + 1.00866) = \mathbf{0.01889\text{ u}}$$ $$\Delta E = \Delta m \cdot c^2 = 0.01889\text{ u} \cdot 1.6605 \times 10^{-27}\text{ kg u}^{-1} \cdot (2.9979 \times 10^8\text{ m s}^{-1})^2 = \mathbf{2.818 \times 10^{-12}\text{ J}} = \mathbf{2.818\text{ pJ}}$$ $$\Delta E\text{ [MeV]} = \frac{2.8182 \times 10^{-12}\text{ J}}{1.6022 \times 10^{-13}\text{ J MeV}^{-1}} = \mathbf{17.59\text{ MeV}}$$
Step (b) – Molar Fusion Energy $\Delta E_{\text{mol}}$:
$$\Delta E_{\text{mol}} = \Delta E \cdot N_A = 2.8182 \times 10^{-12}\text{ J} \cdot 6.0221 \times 10^{23}\text{ mol}^{-1} = 1.697 \times 10^{12}\text{ J mol}^{-1} = \mathbf{1697\text{ GJ mol}^{-1}}$$
Step (c) – $^{131}\text{I}$ Decay Constant $\lambda$ & Remaining Activity after $20.0\text{ d}$:
$$\lambda = \frac{\ln 2}{\tau_{1/2}} = \frac{0.69315}{8.00\text{ d}} = \mathbf{0.08664\text{ d}^{-1}}$$ $$\frac{A(20\text{ d})}{A_0} = e^{-\lambda \cdot t} = \exp\left(-0.08664\text{ d}^{-1} \cdot 20.0\text{ d}\right) = e^{-1.7329} = 0.1768 \implies \mathbf{17.7\%}$$
Guided Example 2 • Ref: L22_The Shell.pdf (Ex. 4 & 6) & Q02_Quizzes_Lecture2.pdf (1st Rep Q6, 3rd Rep Q1)

Hydrogen Balmer Line ($n=5 \rightarrow n=2$), 2D Square Box Degeneracy & $\beta$-Carotene 1D Box

Problem Statement:
(a) The blue Balmer line in the atomic hydrogen emission spectrum has a wavelength of $\lambda = 434.0\text{ nm}$ and originates from the transition $n_i = 5 \rightarrow n_f = 2$ (Quiz Q02, 3rd Rep Q1). Calculate the photon energy $\Delta E$ in $\text{eV}$ and determine the Rydberg constant $\tilde{R}_H$ (in $\text{cm}^{-1}$) from this measurement.
(b) For electrons confined in a 2D square box of side length $L$ (Quiz Q02, 1st Rep Q6), express the energy levels for all combinations of $n_1, n_2 \in \{1, 2\}$ as multiples of $E_0 = \dfrac{h^2}{8 m_e L^2}$.
(c) $\beta$-Carotene has $N_{\pi} = 22$ conjugated $\pi$-electrons delocalized along a chain of length $L = 2.94\text{ nm}$ (`L22_The Shell.pdf`, Ex. 6). Identify the quantum numbers of the HOMO and LUMO and calculate the theoretical HOMO $\rightarrow$ LUMO absorption wavelength $\lambda$ (in $\text{nm}$).

🎙️ Lecturer Board Moderation (Guided Example 2):

"In Step (b), highlight how symmetry creates degeneracy: in a square 2D box ($L_x = L_y = L$), the states $(n_1=1, n_2=2)$ and $(n_1=2, n_2=1)$ both have the exact same energy $5\,E_0$!"

"In Step (c), show how Pauli's exclusion principle connects quantum mechanics to the orange color of carrots: $22$ $\pi$-electrons fill the lowest $11$ levels in pairs ($n_{\text{HOMO}} = 11$, $n_{\text{LUMO}} = 12$), and the energy gap $\Delta E \propto (12^2 - 11^2)/L^2 = 23/L^2$ falls right into the visible blue-green region!"

Step (a) – Balmer Transition $n=5 \rightarrow n=2$ ($\lambda = 434.0\text{ nm}$):
$$\Delta E = \frac{h \cdot c}{\lambda} = \frac{6.6261 \times 10^{-34}\text{ J s} \cdot 2.9979 \times 10^8\text{ m s}^{-1}}{434.0 \times 10^{-9}\text{ m}} = 4.577 \times 10^{-19}\text{ J} = \mathbf{2.857\text{ eV}}$$ $$\tilde{\nu} = \frac{1}{\lambda} = \tilde{R}_H \left(\frac{1}{2^2} - \frac{1}{5^2}\right) = \tilde{R}_H \cdot \frac{21}{100} \implies \tilde{R}_H = \frac{100}{21 \cdot 434.0 \times 10^{-7}\text{ cm}} = \mathbf{109721\text{ cm}^{-1}}$$
Step (b) – 2D Square Box Energy Levels ($E_{n_1,n_2} = (n_1^2 + n_2^2) E_0$):
$$E_{1,1} = (1^2 + 1^2) E_0 = \mathbf{2\,E_0} \quad (\text{non-degenerate zero-point energy})$$ $$E_{1,2} = (1^2 + 2^2) E_0 = \mathbf{5\,E_0} = E_{2,1} \quad (\text{two-fold degenerate level!})$$ $$E_{2,2} = (2^2 + 2^2) E_0 = \mathbf{8\,E_0}$$
Step (c) – $\beta$-Carotene 1D Box ($22$ $\pi$-Electrons, $L = 2.94\text{ nm}$):

By the Pauli principle ($2$ electrons per level): $n_{\text{HOMO}} = \frac{22}{2} = \mathbf{11}$ and $n_{\text{LUMO}} = \mathbf{12}$.

$$\Delta E = \frac{h^2}{8 m_e L^2}\left(12^2 - 11^2\right) = \frac{(6.6261 \times 10^{-34})^2 \cdot 23}{8 \cdot 9.1094 \times 10^{-31} \cdot (2.94 \times 10^{-9})^2} = 1.603 \times 10^{-19}\text{ J}$$ $$\lambda = \frac{h \cdot c}{\Delta E} = \frac{6.6261 \times 10^{-34} \cdot 2.9979 \times 10^8}{1.603 \times 10^{-19}\text{ J}} = 1.239 \times 10^{-6}\text{ m} = \mathbf{1239\text{ nm}}$$

(Note from Lecture Slide/Solutions: The simple free-electron box without bond-length alternation underestimates $\Delta E$ compared to the experimental $497\text{ nm}$ because single/double bond alternation creates an additional Peierls energy gap.)
