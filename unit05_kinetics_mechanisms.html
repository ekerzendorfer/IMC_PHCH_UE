1. Differential & Integrated Rate Laws (Kinetics I)

Rate Definition & Method of Initial Rates:

$$v = -\frac{1}{|\nu_i|}\frac{\text{d}c_i}{\text{d}t} = k \cdot c_A^a \cdot c_B^b \qquad (n_{\text{tot}} = a + b)$$ $$\text{For } c_B = \text{const: } \frac{v_2}{v_1} = \left(\frac{c_{A,2}}{c_{A,1}}\right)^a \implies a = \frac{\ln(v_2/v_1)}{\ln(c_{A,2}/c_{A,1})}$$

First-Order Reaction ($A \rightarrow P$, $v = k \cdot c_t$):

$$c_t = c_0 \cdot e^{-k t} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad k = \frac{1}{t}\ln\left(\frac{c_0}{c_t}\right), \qquad \tau_{1/2} = \frac{\ln 2}{k} \approx \frac{0.6931}{k}$$

Second-Order Reaction ($2A \rightarrow P$ or $A+B \rightarrow P$ with $c_{A,0}=c_{B,0}$):

$$\frac{1}{c_t} - \frac{1}{c_0} = k \cdot t \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad k = \frac{1}{t}\left(\frac{1}{c_t} - \frac{1}{c_0}\right), \qquad \tau_{1/2} = \frac{1}{k \cdot c_0}$$

2. Complex Mechanisms, Pre-Equilibria & QSSA (Kinetics II)

Reversible Drug–Target / Protein–Ligand Binding ($P + L \underset{k_{\text{off}}}{\overset{k_{\text{on}}}{\rightleftharpoons}} PL$):

$$\text{At equilibrium } (v_{\text{on}} = v_{\text{off}}): \quad K_a = \frac{[PL]_{\text{eq}}}{[P]_{\text{eq}}[L]_{\text{eq}}} = \frac{k_{\text{on}}}{k_{\text{off}}}, \qquad K_d = \frac{1}{K_a} = \frac{k_{\text{off}}}{k_{\text{on}}}$$ $$\text{Complex dissociation half-life (1st order): } \tau_{1/2,\text{diss}} = \frac{\ln 2}{k_{\text{off}}}$$

Quasi-Steady-State Approximation (QSSA, $\frac{\text{d}[\text{Intermediate}]}{\text{d}t} \approx 0$):

$$\text{Lindemann } (A + M \underset{k_{-1}}{\overset{k_1}{\rightleftharpoons}} A^* + M, \; A^* \overset{k_2}{\to} P): \quad v = \frac{k_1 k_2 [A][M]}{k_{-1}[M] + k_2}$$ $$\text{Michaelis-Menten } (E + S \underset{k_{-1}}{\overset{k_1}{\rightleftharpoons}} ES \overset{k_2}{\to} E + P): \quad K_M = \frac{k_{-1} + k_2}{k_1}, \quad v_0 = \frac{k_2[E]_0[S]}{K_M + [S]}$$

Guided Example 1 • Ref: 10_Kinetics-I.pdf, Q01 (3rd Rep, Q3 & Q5) & T01 (Q15)

Alkaline Ester Hydrolysis (Saponification): Second-Order Rate Constant $k$ & Half-Life $\tau_{1/2}$ from Titration Data

Problem Statement: The alkaline hydrolysis of ethyl acetate ($\text{CH}_3\text{COOC}_2\text{H}_5 + \text{OH}^- \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{COO}^- + \text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}$) is investigated at $\vartheta = 25.0\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$ using equimolar initial concentrations $c_0(\text{Ester}) = c_0(\text{OH}^-) = 0.0500\text{ mol L}^{-1}$. Because both reactants are present in a $1:1$ ratio, the reaction follows overall second-order kinetics ($v = k \cdot c^2$). At $t_1 = 600\text{ s}$ ($10.0\text{ min}$), titration of a quenched aliquot shows that the remaining $\text{OH}^-$ concentration is $c(600\text{ s}) = 0.0253\text{ mol L}^{-1}$; at $t_2 = 1200\text{ s}$ ($20.0\text{ min}$), it is $c(1200\text{ s}) = 0.0169\text{ mol L}^{-1}$.
(a) Verify that the data follow a second-order rate law by computing $k$ at both $t_1 = 600\text{ s}$ and $t_2 = 1200\text{ s}$ (including proper units!).
(b) Calculate the initial half-life $\tau_{1/2}$ (in $\text{s}$ and $\text{min}$) for $c_0 = 0.0500\text{ mol L}^{-1}$.
(c) Calculate the remaining ester concentration $c(t)$ after $t = 30.0\text{ min}$ ($1800\text{ s}$).

🎙️ Lecturer Board Moderation (Guided Example 1):

"Point out why we calculate $k$ at two different time points when a table is given in Prof. Kerschbaumer's quizzes (like Q01 3rd Rep Q5 / T01 Q15): if $k = \frac{1}{t}(\frac{1}{c_t} - \frac{1}{c_0})$ remains constant across time points, that proves 2nd-order behavior!"

"Also emphasize the contrast between 1st and 2nd order half-lives: in 1st order (radioactive decay), $\tau_{1/2}$ is constant. In 2nd order, $\tau_{1/2} = \frac{1}{k \cdot c_0}$ doubles every time the concentration drops by half because collisions become much rarer at low concentrations!"

Step (a) – Second-Order Rate Constant $k$ from Experimental Data:

Using $k = \dfrac{1}{t}\left(\dfrac{1}{c_t} - \dfrac{1}{c_0}\right)$ with $\dfrac{1}{c_0} = \dfrac{1}{0.0500\text{ mol L}^{-1}} = 20.00\text{ L mol}^{-1}$:

$$k(600\text{ s}) = \frac{1}{600\text{ s}}\left(\frac{1}{0.0253\text{ mol L}^{-1}} - 20.00\text{ L mol}^{-1}\right) = \frac{39.526 - 20.00}{600\text{ s}} = \mathbf{0.03254\text{ L mol}^{-1}\text{ s}^{-1}}$$ $$k(1200\text{ s}) = \frac{1}{1200\text{ s}}\left(\frac{1}{0.0169\text{ mol L}^{-1}} - 20.00\text{ L mol}^{-1}\right) = \frac{59.172 - 20.00}{1200\text{ s}} = \mathbf{0.03264\text{ L mol}^{-1}\text{ s}^{-1}}$$

Mean rate constant: $\bar{k} = \mathbf{3.26 \times 10^{-2}\text{ L mol}^{-1}\text{ s}^{-1}}$ (constant value confirms 2nd order!).

Step (b) – Second-Order Half-Life $\tau_{1/2}$:
$$\tau_{1/2} = \frac{1}{k \cdot c_0} = \frac{1}{0.0326\text{ L mol}^{-1}\text{ s}^{-1} \cdot 0.0500\text{ mol L}^{-1}} = \mathbf{613.5\text{ s}} = \mathbf{10.2\text{ min}}$$
Step (c) – Remaining Concentration after $t = 1800\text{ s}$ ($30.0\text{ min}$):
$$\frac{1}{c_t} = \frac{1}{c_0} + k \cdot t = 20.00\text{ L mol}^{-1} + 0.0326\text{ L mol}^{-1}\text{ s}^{-1} \cdot 1800\text{ s} = 78.68\text{ L mol}^{-1}$$ $$c(1800\text{ s}) = \frac{1}{78.68\text{ L mol}^{-1}} = \mathbf{0.01271\text{ mol L}^{-1}}$$
Guided Example 2 • Ref: 12_Kinetics-II.pdf & T01 (Q17)

Reversible Drug–Protein Binding ($K_a, K_d$) & Quasi-Steady-State Approximation (QSSA)

Problem Statement: In biophysical pharmacology, a kinase inhibitor $L$ binds reversibly to its target enzyme $E$ according to $E + L \underset{k_{\text{off}}}{\overset{k_{\text{on}}}{\rightleftharpoons}} EL$ with a second-order association rate constant $k_{\text{on}} = 4.00 \times 10^6\text{ L mol}^{-1}\text{ s}^{-1}$ and a first-order dissociation rate constant $k_{\text{off}} = 0.0200\text{ s}^{-1}$. When the natural substrate $S$ binds instead ($E + S \underset{k_{-1}}{\overset{k_1}{\rightleftharpoons}} ES \overset{k_2}{\longrightarrow} E + P$), the elementary rate constants are $k_1 = 2.50 \times 10^7\text{ L mol}^{-1}\text{ s}^{-1}$, $k_{-1} = 500\text{ s}^{-1}$, and catalytic turnover number $k_2 = k_{\text{cat}} = 150\text{ s}^{-1}$.
(a) Calculate the thermodynamic association constant $K_a$ (in $\text{L mol}^{-1}$), the dissociation constant $K_d$ (in $\text{nmol L}^{-1}$), and the residence half-life $\tau_{1/2,\text{diss}}$ of the drug–target complex $EL$.
(b) Apply Bodenstein's Quasi-Steady-State Approximation ($\text{d}[ES]/\text{d}t \approx 0$) to calculate the Michaelis constant $K_M$ of the enzyme for substrate $S$ (in $\mu\text{mol L}^{-1}$).
(c) For a total enzyme concentration $[E]_0 = 2.00 \times 10^{-8}\text{ mol L}^{-1}$ and substrate concentration $[S] = 52.0\text{ }\mu\text{mol L}^{-1}$, calculate $v_{\max}$ and the initial catalytic rate $v_0$ (in $\mu\text{mol L}^{-1}\text{ s}^{-1}$).

🎙️ Lecturer Board Moderation (Guided Example 2):

"Show the students the physical meaning of the units: $k_{\text{on}}$ is 2nd order ($\text{L mol}^{-1}\text{ s}^{-1}$) and $k_{\text{off}}$ is 1st order ($\text{s}^{-1}$). Therefore, $K_d = k_{\text{off}} / k_{\text{on}}$ automatically has the unit $\text{mol L}^{-1}$ — it is literally the ligand concentration at which exactly $50\%$ of all protein binding sites are occupied!"

"Then point out the difference between true pre-equilibrium ($K_d = k_{-1}/k_1$) and Steady-State ($K_M = (k_{-1} + k_2)/k_1$): only when $k_2 \ll k_{-1}$ does $K_M$ simplify to $K_d$!"

Step (a) – Drug Binding Constants $K_a, K_d$ & Complex Residence Half-Life:
$$K_a = \frac{k_{\text{on}}}{k_{\text{off}}} = \frac{4.00 \times 10^6\text{ L mol}^{-1}\text{ s}^{-1}}{0.0200\text{ s}^{-1}} = \mathbf{2.00 \times 10^8\text{ L mol}^{-1}}$$ $$K_d = \frac{k_{\text{off}}}{k_{\text{on}}} = \frac{1}{K_a} = 5.00 \times 10^{-9}\text{ mol L}^{-1} = \mathbf{5.00\text{ nmol L}^{-1}}$$ $$\tau_{1/2,\text{diss}} = \frac{\ln 2}{k_{\text{off}}} = \frac{0.69315}{0.0200\text{ s}^{-1}} = \mathbf{34.66\text{ s}}$$
Step (b) – Steady-State Condition $\frac{\text{d}[ES]}{\text{d}t} = k_1[E][S] - (k_{-1} + k_2)[ES] \approx 0$:
$$K_M = \frac{k_{-1} + k_2}{k_1} = \frac{500\text{ s}^{-1} + 150\text{ s}^{-1}}{2.50 \times 10^7\text{ L mol}^{-1}\text{ s}^{-1}} = 2.60 \times 10^{-5}\text{ mol L}^{-1} = \mathbf{26.0\text{ }\mu\text{mol L}^{-1}}$$
Step (c) – Maximum Rate $v_{\max}$ & Initial Rate $v_0$:
$$v_{\max} = k_2 \cdot [E]_0 = 150\text{ s}^{-1} \cdot 2.00 \times 10^{-8}\text{ mol L}^{-1} = 3.00 \times 10^{-6}\text{ mol L}^{-1}\text{ s}^{-1} = \mathbf{3.00\text{ }\mu\text{mol L}^{-1}\text{ s}^{-1}}$$ $$v_0 = \frac{v_{\max} \cdot [S]}{K_M + [S]} = \frac{3.00\text{ }\mu\text{mol L}^{-1}\text{ s}^{-1} \cdot 52.0\text{ }\mu\text{mol L}^{-1}}{26.0 + 52.0\text{ }\mu\text{mol L}^{-1}} = \mathbf{2.00\text{ }\mu\text{mol L}^{-1}\text{ s}^{-1}}$$
