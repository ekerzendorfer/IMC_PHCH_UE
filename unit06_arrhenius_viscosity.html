🧠 The 5 Fundamental "By Heart" Formulas (Required without Formula Sheet in Exams!)
$$\rho = \frac{m}{V}$$
$$n = \frac{m}{M}$$
$$c = \frac{n}{V}$$
$$p \cdot V = n \cdot R \cdot T$$
$$x_i = \frac{n_i}{n_{\text{tot}}}$$

1. Reaction Order from Unit of $k$ & Arrhenius Law (Kinetics III)

General Unit of Rate Constant $k$ for Overall Order $n$:

$$[k] = \frac{[v]}{[c]^n} = \text{mol}^{1-n}\cdot \text{L}^{n-1}\cdot \text{s}^{-1} = \text{M}^{1-n}\cdot \text{s}^{-1}$$

Arrhenius Equation & Linearized Form ($R = 8.3145\text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{ K}^{-1}$):

$$k = A \cdot \exp\left(-\frac{E_a}{R \cdot T}\right) \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \ln k = \ln A - \frac{E_a}{R} \cdot \frac{1}{T}$$

Two-Point Arrhenius Equations (for $k$ and Half-Life $\tau_{1/2} \propto 1/k$):

$$\ln\left(\frac{k_2}{k_1}\right) = \ln\left(\frac{\tau_{1/2}(T_1)}{\tau_{1/2}(T_2)}\right) = \frac{E_a}{R}\left(\frac{1}{T_1} - \frac{1}{T_2}\right) = \frac{E_a}{R}\left(\frac{T_2 - T_1}{T_1 \cdot T_2}\right)$$ $$E_a = \frac{R \cdot T_1 \cdot T_2}{T_2 - T_1} \cdot \ln\left(\frac{k_2}{k_1}\right), \qquad A = k_1 \cdot \exp\left(\frac{E_a}{R \cdot T_1}\right)$$

2. Dynamic ($\eta$) vs. Kinematic Viscosity ($\nu$) & Viscometry

Newton's Shear Law & Kinematic Viscosity Definition:

$$\tau = \frac{F}{A} = \eta \cdot \frac{\text{d}v}{\text{d}y}, \qquad \nu = \frac{\eta}{\rho} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \eta = \nu \cdot \rho$$

Hagen–Poiseuille Law & Ostwald Capillary Viscometer:

$$\frac{\text{d}V}{\text{d}t} = \frac{\pi \cdot r^4 \cdot \Delta p}{8 \cdot \eta \cdot l}, \qquad \text{With hydrostatic } \Delta p = \rho \cdot g \cdot h:$$ $$\nu = \frac{\eta}{\rho} = K_{\text{visc}} \cdot t \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \frac{\eta_A}{\eta_B} = \frac{\rho_A \cdot t_A}{\rho_B \cdot t_B}$$

Arrhenius–Andrade Temperature Dependence of Viscosity: $\eta(T) = \eta_0 \cdot \exp\left(\frac{E_{\text{visc}}}{R \cdot T}\right)$ (liquids become less viscous as $T$ rises!).

⚡ Reaction Order Quick-Check from $[k]$:
  • $n = 0$: $[k] = \text{mol L}^{-1}\text{ s}^{-1} = \text{mol dm}^{-3}\text{ s}^{-1}$
  • $n = 1$: $[k] = \text{s}^{-1}$ (or $\text{min}^{-1}, \text{h}^{-1}$)
  • $n = 2$: $[k] = \text{L mol}^{-1}\text{ s}^{-1} = \text{dm}^3\text{ mol}^{-1}\text{ s}^{-1} = \text{M}^{-1}\text{ s}^{-1}$
  • $n = 3$: $[k] = \text{L}^2\text{ mol}^{-2}\text{ s}^{-1} = \text{dm}^6\text{ mol}^{-2}\text{ s}^{-1}$
  • $n = 1.5$: $[k] = \text{L}^{0.5}\text{ mol}^{-0.5}\text{ s}^{-1}$ (always check exponent of $\text{L}$: $n = \text{exp}(\text{L}) + 1$!)
⚠️ Golden CGS $\leftrightarrow$ SI Conversions for Viscosity:
  • Dynamic Viscosity $\eta$: SI unit is $\text{Pa s} = \text{kg m}^{-1}\text{ s}^{-1} = \text{N s m}^{-2}$
  • $1\text{ Poise (P)} = 1\text{ g cm}^{-1}\text{ s}^{-1} = 0.1\text{ Pa s} = 100\text{ mPa s}$
  • $1\text{ centipoise (cP)} = 10^{-2}\text{ P} = 1\text{ mPa s} = 10^{-3}\text{ Pa s}$
  • Kinematic Viscosity $\nu$: SI unit is $\text{m}^2\text{ s}^{-1}$
  • $1\text{ Stokes (St)} = 1\text{ cm}^2\text{ s}^{-1} = 10^{-4}\text{ m}^2\text{ s}^{-1} = 100\text{ mm}^2\text{ s}^{-1}$
  • $1\text{ centistokes (cSt)} = 10^{-2}\text{ St} = 1\text{ mm}^2\text{ s}^{-1} = 10^{-6}\text{ m}^2\text{ s}^{-1}$
  • Density $\rho$: $1\text{ g cm}^{-3} = 1000\text{ kg m}^{-3}$

Guided Example 1 • Ref: 13_Kinetics-III.pdf, Q01 (3rd Rep, Q4) & T01 (Q16)

Reaction Order from the Unit of $k$, Two-Point Arrhenius Equation ($E_a, A$) & Half-Life Shift

Problem Statement: The thermal decomposition of an active pharmaceutical ingredient in solution has a rate constant of $k_1 = 1.40 \times 10^{-4}\text{ s}^{-1}$ at $\vartheta_1 = 25.0\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$ and $k_2 = 1.12 \times 10^{-3}\text{ s}^{-1}$ at $\vartheta_2 = 45.0\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$.
(a) Deduce the overall reaction order $n$ from the unit of $k$, and calculate the activation energy $E_a$ (in $\text{kJ mol}^{-1}$) using the two-point Arrhenius equation.
(b) Calculate the pre-exponential frequency factor $A$ (with proper units!).
(c) Predict the rate constant $k_3$ and the reaction half-life $\tau_{1/2}$ (in $\text{min}$) under accelerated stability testing at $\vartheta_3 = 60.0\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$.

🎙️ Lecturer Board Moderation (Guided Example 1):

"First, show the universal formula $[k] = \text{mol}^{1-n}\text{ L}^{n-1}\text{ s}^{-1}$. Whenever $\text{mol}$ and $\text{L}$ cancel out completely ($[k] = \text{s}^{-1}$), $1 - n = 0 \implies n = 1$ (first order). In Quiz Q01 (3rd Rep, Q4), students had to identify orders directly from units like $\text{L}^2\text{ mol}^{-2}\text{ s}^{-1}$ ($n=3$) or $\text{mol L}^{-1}\text{ s}^{-1}$ ($n=0$)."

"Second, warn them about the two deadly Arrhenius traps: (1) NEVER plug Celsius temperatures into $\frac{1}{T_1} - \frac{1}{T_2}$ — always convert to Kelvin ($+273.15$) first! (2) $R = 8.3145\text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{ K}^{-1}$ yields $E_a$ in $\text{J mol}^{-1}$, typically around $50\,000$ to $100\,000\text{ J mol}^{-1}$ ($50\text{–}100\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$)."

Step (a) – Reaction Order $n$ & Activation Energy $E_a$:

From $[k] = \text{s}^{-1} = \text{mol}^{1-n}\text{ L}^{n-1}\text{ s}^{-1} \implies 1 - n = 0 \implies \mathbf{n = 1}$ (first-order reaction).

Convert temperatures to Kelvin: $T_1 = 298.15\text{ K}$, $T_2 = 318.15\text{ K}$. With $\frac{k_2}{k_1} = \frac{1.12 \times 10^{-3}}{1.40 \times 10^{-4}} = 8.00$:

$$E_a = \frac{R \cdot T_1 \cdot T_2}{T_2 - T_1} \cdot \ln\left(\frac{k_2}{k_1}\right) = \frac{8.3145\text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{ K}^{-1} \cdot 298.15\text{ K} \cdot 318.15\text{ K}}{20.00\text{ K}} \cdot \ln(8.00)$$ $$E_a = 39434\text{ J mol}^{-1} \cdot 2.07944 = 8.200 \times 10^4\text{ J mol}^{-1} = \mathbf{82.00\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}}$$
Step (b) – Pre-Exponential Frequency Factor $A$:

Remember to use $E_a = 82000\text{ J mol}^{-1}$ (in Joules!) in the exponent $\frac{E_a}{R \cdot T_1} = \frac{82000}{8.3145 \cdot 298.15} = 33.078$:

$$A = k_1 \cdot \exp\left(\frac{E_a}{R \cdot T_1}\right) = 1.40 \times 10^{-4}\text{ s}^{-1} \cdot e^{33.078} = \mathbf{3.29 \times 10^{10}\text{ s}^{-1}}$$
Step (c) – Rate Constant $k_3$ & Half-Life $\tau_{1/2}$ at $\vartheta_3 = 60.0\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$ ($T_3 = 333.15\text{ K}$):
$$k_3 = k_2 \cdot \exp\left[\frac{E_a}{R}\left(\frac{1}{T_2} - \frac{1}{T_3}\right)\right] = 1.12 \times 10^{-3}\text{ s}^{-1} \cdot \exp\left[\frac{82000}{8.3145}\left(\frac{1}{318.15} - \frac{1}{333.15}\right)\right] = \mathbf{4.52 \times 10^{-3}\text{ s}^{-1}}$$ $$\tau_{1/2}(60\text{ }^\circ\text{C}) = \frac{\ln 2}{k_3} = \frac{0.69315}{4.52 \times 10^{-3}\text{ s}^{-1}} = 153.4\text{ s} = \mathbf{2.56\text{ min}}$$
Guided Example 2 • Ref: 14_Viscosity.pdf & T01 (Q18)

Dynamic ($\eta$) vs. Kinematic Viscosity ($\nu$) & Ostwald Capillary Viscometer

Problem Statement: In an Ostwald capillary viscometer thermostatted at $\vartheta = 20.0\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$, pure water ($\rho_w = 0.9982\text{ g cm}^{-3}$, dynamic viscosity $\eta_w = 1.002\text{ cP}$) requires a flow time of $t_w = 85.0\text{ s}$ to pass between the two timing marks. When the same viscometer is filled with an equal volume of pure ethanol ($\rho_{\text{EtOH}} = 0.7893\text{ g cm}^{-3}$), the measured flow time is $t_{\text{EtOH}} = 129.0\text{ s}$.
(a) Convert the dynamic viscosity of water $\eta_w = 1.002\text{ cP}$ into $\text{Poise (P)}$ and strict SI units ($\text{Pa s}$), and calculate the viscometer apparatus constant $K_{\text{visc}}$ (in $\text{cSt s}^{-1} = \text{mm}^2\text{ s}^{-2}$).
(b) Calculate the dynamic viscosity $\eta_{\text{EtOH}}$ of ethanol in $\text{mPa s}$ ($\text{cP}$) and in $\text{Pa s}$.
(c) Calculate the kinematic viscosity $\nu_{\text{EtOH}}$ of ethanol in $\text{cSt}$ ($\text{mm}^2\text{ s}^{-1}$), in $\text{St}$ ($\text{cm}^2\text{ s}^{-1}$), and in strict SI units ($\text{m}^2\text{ s}^{-1}$).

🎙️ Lecturer Board Moderation (Guided Example 2):

"Ask the class why density $\rho$ appears in the Ostwald viscometer equation $\frac{\eta_A}{\eta_B} = \frac{\rho_A t_A}{\rho_B t_B}$: because in a gravity-fed capillary viscometer, the liquid's own hydrostatic weight $\Delta p = \rho \cdot g \cdot h$ drives the flow! A denser liquid pushes itself faster through the capillary."

"Give them the two-second unit anchor: Water at $20\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$ has $\eta \approx 1\text{ cP} = 1\text{ mPa s} = 10^{-3}\text{ Pa s}$ and $\nu \approx 1\text{ cSt} = 1\text{ mm}^2\text{ s}^{-1} = 10^{-6}\text{ m}^2\text{ s}^{-1}$. If you remember water at $20\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$, you can never mix up $\text{cP}/\text{Pa s}$ or $\text{cSt}/\text{m}^2\text{ s}^{-1}$ in the exam!"

Step (a) – Water Viscosity in SI & Viscometer Constant $K_{\text{visc}}$:
$$\eta_w = 1.002\text{ cP} = \mathbf{0.01002\text{ P}} = 1.002\text{ mPa s} = \mathbf{1.002 \times 10^{-3}\text{ Pa s}}$$ $$\nu_w = \frac{\eta_w\text{ [mPa s]}}{\rho_w\text{ [g cm}^{-3}\text{]}} = \frac{1.002}{0.9982} = 1.0038\text{ cSt (mm}^2\text{ s}^{-1}\text{)}$$ $$K_{\text{visc}} = \frac{\nu_w}{t_w} = \frac{1.0038\text{ cSt}}{85.0\text{ s}} = \mathbf{0.01181\text{ cSt s}^{-1}} \quad (\text{or } 1.181 \times 10^{-8}\text{ m}^2\text{ s}^{-2})$$
Step (b) – Dynamic Viscosity $\eta_{\text{EtOH}}$ via Ostwald Ratio:
$$\eta_{\text{EtOH}} = \eta_w \cdot \frac{\rho_{\text{EtOH}} \cdot t_{\text{EtOH}}}{\rho_w \cdot t_w} = 1.002\text{ mPa s} \cdot \frac{0.7893 \cdot 129.0}{0.9982 \cdot 85.0} = \mathbf{1.202\text{ mPa s (cP)}} = \mathbf{1.202 \times 10^{-3}\text{ Pa s}}$$
Step (c) – Kinematic Viscosity $\nu_{\text{EtOH}}$ in CGS & SI Units:
$$\nu_{\text{EtOH}} = \frac{\eta_{\text{EtOH}}}{\rho_{\text{EtOH}}} = \frac{1.202\text{ mPa s}}{0.7893\text{ g cm}^{-3}} = \mathbf{1.523\text{ cSt (mm}^2\text{ s}^{-1}\text{)}} = \mathbf{0.01523\text{ St (cm}^2\text{ s}^{-1}\text{)}}$$ $$\nu_{\text{EtOH}}\text{ (SI)} = \frac{1.202 \times 10^{-3}\text{ Pa s}}{789.3\text{ kg m}^{-3}} = \mathbf{1.523 \times 10^{-6}\text{ m}^2\text{ s}^{-1}}$$
