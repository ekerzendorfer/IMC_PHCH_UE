⚠️ The #1 Unit Trap in Chemical Thermodynamics:
Enthalpies (\(\Delta_r H^\circ\)) and Gibbs energies (\(\Delta_r G^\circ\)) are almost always tabulated in \(\text{kJ mol}^{-1}\), whereas entropies (\(\Delta_r S^\circ\)), heat capacities (\(\Delta_r C_p\)), and the gas constant \(R = 8.314\) are in \(\text{J mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}\)! Always convert to the same energy unit (\(\text{J}\) or \(\text{kJ}\)) before adding or taking \(\exp(-\Delta_r G^\circ / RT)\).

2nd & 3rd Law (L03 Slide 24 & L06 Slide 6)

  • Boltzmann Entropy: \(S = k \cdot \ln W\)  (\(k = 1.3806 \cdot 10^{-23}\text{ J K}^{-1}\))
  • Isothermal Volume/Pressure Change: \(\Delta S_{\text{sys}} = nR\ln\frac{V_f}{V_i} = nR\ln\frac{p_i}{p_f}\)
  • Temperature Change: \(\Delta S = n C_{p,m}\ln\frac{T_f}{T_i}\) (at const. \(p\)) or \(n C_{V,m}\ln\frac{T_f}{T_i}\) (at const. \(V\))
  • Reversible vs. Vacuum Expansion: Reversible: \(\Delta S_{\text{tot}} = 0 \implies \Delta S_{\text{sur}} = -\Delta S_{\text{sys}}\). Irreversible (\(p_{\text{ex}}=0\)): \(\Delta S_{\text{sur}} = 0 \implies \Delta S_{\text{tot}} = \Delta S_{\text{sys}}\).
  • Ideal Gas Mixing: \(\Delta_{\text{mix}}S = -n_{\text{tot}} R \sum x_i \ln x_i > 0\)  |  \(\Delta_{\text{mix}}G = -T \cdot \Delta_{\text{mix}}S < 0\)

Kirchhoff, Gibbs & Equilibrium (L04 Slide 30)

  • Kirchhoff's Laws (\(\Delta_r C_p = \text{const.}\)):
    \(\Delta_r H^\circ(T_2) = \Delta_r H^\circ(T_1) + \Delta_r C_p \cdot (T_2 - T_1)\)
    \(\Delta_r S^\circ(T_2) = \Delta_r S^\circ(T_1) + \Delta_r C_p \cdot \ln\left(\frac{T_2}{T_1}\right)\)
  • Gibbs-Helmholtz Equation: \(\Delta_r G^\circ = \Delta_r H^\circ - T \cdot \Delta_r S^\circ\)
  • Equilibrium Constant: \(\Delta_r G^\circ = -RT\ln K_p^{\text{th}} \implies K_p^{\text{th}} = \exp\left(-\frac{\Delta_r G^\circ}{RT}\right)\)
  • Non-Equilibrium (\(\Delta_r G\)): \(\Delta_r G = \Delta_r G^\circ + RT\ln Q = RT\ln\left(\frac{Q}{K_p^{\text{th}}}\right)\)

Modeling Example A • Entropy & Gibbs Energy of Mixing

Mixing of Oxygen and Nitrogen: \(\Delta_{\text{mix}}S\) and \(\Delta_{\text{mix}}G\)

Ref: Try-Test 1 (Q5) • L06 Slides 6–7
Problem: \(n(\text{O}_2) = 2.00\text{ mol}\) of Oxygen and \(n(\text{N}_2) = 8.00\text{ mol}\) of Nitrogen are mixed isothermally at \(\vartheta = 27\text{ }^\circ\text{C}\) (\(T = 300.15\text{ K}\)) and constant total pressure. Assume both gases behave ideally.
a) Determine the total amount of gas \(n_{\text{tot}}\) and the mole fractions \(x(\text{O}_2)\) and \(x(\text{N}_2)\).
b) Calculate the entropy of mixing \(\Delta_{\text{mix}}S\) (in \(\text{J K}^{-1}\)).
c) Calculate the Gibbs energy of mixing \(\Delta_{\text{mix}}G\) (in \(\text{kJ}\)) and comment on the signs.
🎙️ Spoken Board Moderation (English Phrases):

• "When two ideal gases mix at constant temperature, there is no enthalpy of mixing (\(\Delta_{\text{mix}}H = 0\)) because ideal gas particles do not interact."

• "The driving force is purely entropic: Since mole fractions \(x_i\) are always smaller than 1, their natural logarithms \(\ln x_i\) are negative. Combined with the minus sign in front of the formula, \(\Delta_{\text{mix}}S\) is ALWAYS positive!"

• "Then, from \(\Delta_{\text{mix}}G = \Delta_{\text{mix}}H - T\Delta_{\text{mix}}S = -T\Delta_{\text{mix}}S\), we immediately get a negative Gibbs energy of mixing — proving that gases mix spontaneously."

Step a: Total Amount & Mole Fractions

\(n_{\text{tot}} = 2.00\text{ mol} + 8.00\text{ mol} = 10.0\text{ mol}\)
\(x(\text{O}_2) = \frac{2.00}{10.0} = \mathbf{0.200}\)  |  \(x(\text{N}_2) = \frac{8.00}{10.0} = \mathbf{0.800}\)

Step b: Entropy of Mixing \(\Delta_{\text{mix}}S\)

Watch the negative values of \(\ln(0.200) = -1.6094\) and \(\ln(0.800) = -0.2231\):

\(\Delta_{\text{mix}}S = -n_{\text{tot}} \cdot R \cdot \left(x_{\text{O}_2}\ln x_{\text{O}_2} + x_{\text{N}_2}\ln x_{\text{N}_2}\right)\)
\(\Delta_{\text{mix}}S = -10.0\text{ mol} \cdot 8.314\text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1} \cdot \left(0.200 \cdot (-1.6094) + 0.800 \cdot (-0.2231)\right)\)
\(\Delta_{\text{mix}}S = -83.14\text{ J K}^{-1} \cdot (-0.5004) = \mathbf{+41.6\text{ J K}^{-1}}\)

Step c: Gibbs Energy of Mixing \(\Delta_{\text{mix}}G\) at \(T = 300.15\text{ K}\)

\(\Delta_{\text{mix}}G = -T \cdot \Delta_{\text{mix}}S = -300.15\text{ K} \cdot 41.60\text{ J K}^{-1} = -12486\text{ J} = \mathbf{-12.5\text{ kJ}}\)

Check: Mixing is always spontaneous (\(\Delta_{\text{mix}}G < 0\)) and increases disorder (\(\Delta_{\text{mix}}S > 0\)).

Modeling Example B • Chemical Equilibrium & Reaction Quotient \(Q\)

Water-Gas Shift Equilibrium: \(\Delta_r G^\circ\), \(K_p^{\text{th}}\) and Direction of Reaction

Ref: L04 Slides 13–15, 26 • Try-Test 1 Q8
Problem: Consider the gas-phase reaction \(\text{CO}(g) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(g) \rightleftharpoons \text{CO}_2(g) + \text{H}_2(g)\) at \(T = 700\text{ K}\).
At \(700\text{ K}\), the thermochemical data are \(\Delta_r H^\circ_{700} = -37.8\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}\) and \(\Delta_r S^\circ_{700} = -35.2\text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}\).
a) Calculate the standard molar Gibbs energy \(\Delta_r G^\circ_{700}\) (in \(\text{kJ mol}^{-1}\)).
b) Calculate the thermodynamic equilibrium constant \(K_p^{\text{th}}\) at \(700\text{ K}\).
c) A reactor at \(700\text{ K}\) contains partial pressures \(p(\text{CO}_2) = 2.0\text{ bar}\), \(p(\text{H}_2) = 2.0\text{ bar}\), \(p(\text{CO}) = 0.20\text{ bar}\), and \(p(\text{H}_2\text{O}) = 0.10\text{ bar}\). Calculate \(Q\), \(\Delta_r G\), and state the direction of reaction.
🎙️ Spoken Board Moderation (English Phrases):

• "Here is the classic exam trap twice in one problem: In Step a, \(\Delta_r H^\circ\) is in kilojoules, while \(\Delta_r S^\circ\) is in Joules per Kelvin. Divide \(\Delta_r S^\circ\) by 1000 before subtracting!"

• "In Step b, when we plug \(\Delta_r G^\circ\) into the exponential \(K_p = \exp(-\Delta_r G^\circ / RT)\), we must convert \(\Delta_r G^\circ\) back to Joules per mole because \(R = 8.314\) is in Joules!"

• "In Step c, we compare the reaction quotient \(Q\) with \(K_p\). Since \(Q = 200\) is larger than \(K_p = 9.59\), we have too many products, so \(\Delta_r G > 0\) and the reaction proceeds backwards (from right to left)."

Step a: Standard Gibbs Energy \(\Delta_r G^\circ_{700}\) (\(\text{J} \to \text{kJ}\))

Convert \(\Delta_r S^\circ_{700} = -35.2\text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1} = -0.0352\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}\):

\(\Delta_r G^\circ_{700} = \Delta_r H^\circ_{700} - T \cdot \Delta_r S^\circ_{700} = -37.8\text{ kJ mol}^{-1} - 700\text{ K} \cdot (-0.0352\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1})\)
\(\Delta_r G^\circ_{700} = -37.8 + 24.64 = \mathbf{-13.16\text{ kJ mol}^{-1} = -13160\text{ J mol}^{-1}}\)

Step b: Thermodynamic Equilibrium Constant \(K_p^{\text{th}}\)

Use \(\Delta_r G^\circ\) in \(\text{J mol}^{-1}\) in the exponent:

\(K_p^{\text{th}} = \exp\left(-\frac{\Delta_r G^\circ_{700}}{R \cdot T}\right) = \exp\left(-\frac{-13160\text{ J mol}^{-1}}{8.314\text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1} \cdot 700\text{ K}}\right) = \exp(+2.2612) = \mathbf{9.59}\)

Step c: Reaction Quotient \(Q\), \(\Delta_r G\) and Shift Direction

\(Q = \frac{p(\text{CO}_2) \cdot p(\text{H}_2)}{p(\text{CO}) \cdot p(\text{H}_2\text{O})} = \frac{2.0 \cdot 2.0}{0.20 \cdot 0.10} = \mathbf{200}\)
\(\Delta_r G = R \cdot T \cdot \ln\left(\frac{Q}{K_p^{\text{th}}}\right) = 8.314 \cdot 700 \cdot \ln\left(\frac{200}{9.59}\right) = +17680\text{ J mol}^{-1} = \mathbf{+17.7\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}}\)

Conclusion: Since \(Q > K_p^{\text{th}}\) (and \(\Delta_r G > 0\) for the forward reaction), the system reacts spontaneously from right to left (forming reactants).
