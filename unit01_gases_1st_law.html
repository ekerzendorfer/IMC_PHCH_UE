⚠️ Exam Rule ("By Heart" Formulae — L00 Intro Slide 2): In all quizzes and the final exam, you must know these 5 basic equations by heart (they are not printed on the exam formula sheet): \(\rho = \frac{m}{V}\)  |  \(n = \frac{m}{M}\)  |  \(c = \frac{n}{V}\)  |  \(p \cdot V = n \cdot R \cdot T\)  |  \(x_i = \frac{n_i}{n_{\text{tot}}}\)  (and \(p_i = x_i \cdot p_{\text{tot}}\))

Constants & Unit Conversions

  • \(R = 8.314\text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1} = 8.314\text{ Pa m}^3\text{mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}\)
  • \(R = 0.08314\text{ dm}^3\text{ bar mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}\)
  • \(R = 0.08205\text{ L atm mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}\)
  • \(1\text{ bar} = 10^5\text{ Pa} = 100\text{ kPa}\)  |  \(1\text{ L}\cdot\text{bar} = 100\text{ J}\)
  • \(1\text{ atm} = 1.01325\text{ bar} = 760\text{ Torr}\)
  • \(T(\text{K}) = \vartheta(^\circ\text{C}) + 273.15\)

Formulas Provided on Exam Sheet (L01 Slide 28 & L02 Slide 34)

  • Mean speed: \(v_{\text{mean}} = \sqrt{\frac{8RT}{\pi M}} \implies \frac{v_A}{v_B} = \sqrt{\frac{M_B}{M_A}}\)
  • Van der Waals: \(p = \frac{nRT}{V - nb} - a\frac{n^2}{V^2}\)  |  \(Z = \frac{p_{\text{real}}}{p_{\text{id}}} = \frac{V_{\text{real}}}{V_{\text{id}}}\)
  • Critical data: \(T_c = \frac{8a}{27Rb}\),  \(p_c = \frac{a}{27b^2}\),  \(V_c = 3b\)
  • Work (1st Law): \(w_{\text{irrev}} = -p_{\text{ex}}\Delta V\)  |  \(w_{\text{rev}} = -nRT\ln\frac{V_f}{V_i}\)
  • Heat capacity & Adiabat: \(C_{p,m} - C_{V,m} = R\),  \(\gamma = \frac{C_{p,m}}{C_{V,m}}\),  \(p_i V_i^\gamma = p_f V_f^\gamma\)

Modeling Example A • Real Gases & Van der Waals

Chlorine Gas under High Pressure: Ideal vs. Real Pressure & Compression Factor \(Z\)

Ref: L01 Slides 19–25 • Quiz 1 (Rep 1, Q2)
Problem: \(n = 2.00\text{ mol}\) of Chlorine gas (\(\text{Cl}_2\)) are confined in a steel vessel of volume \(V = 3.00\text{ L}\) at \(T = 350\text{ K}\).
Van der Waals parameters for \(\text{Cl}_2\): \(a = 6.260\text{ atm L}^2\text{mol}^{-2}\) and \(b = 0.0542\text{ L mol}^{-1}\).
a) Calculate the pressure \(p_{\text{id}}\) assuming perfect gas behaviour.
b) Calculate the real pressure \(p_{\text{vdW}}\) assuming \(\text{Cl}_2\) behaves as a Van der Waals gas.
c) Calculate the compression factor \(Z\) and decide which type of intermolecular forces predominate.
🎙️ Spoken Board Moderation (English Phrases):

• "Before touching the calculator, let's check the units of the Van der Waals constants \(a\) and \(b\). Notice that \(a\) is given in \(\text{atm}\cdot\text{L}^2\cdot\text{mol}^{-2}\)."

• "To make the units cancel out cleanly, we do NOT use \(R = 8.314\), but we pick \(R = 0.08205\text{ L}\cdot\text{atm}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}\cdot\text{K}^{-1}\) from our formula sheet."

• "In the Van der Waals equation, the first term corrects for the excluded volume \(n\cdot b\) (repulsion), while the second term subtracts \(a\cdot n^2/V^2\) for intermolecular attraction."

Step a: Ideal Gas Law (Choice of \(R\))

Since \(V\) is in \(\text{L}\) and \(a, b\) use \(\text{atm}\) and \(\text{L}\), we select \(R = 0.08205\text{ L atm mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}\):

\(p_{\text{id}} = \frac{n \cdot R \cdot T}{V} = \frac{2.00\text{ mol} \cdot 0.08205\text{ L atm mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1} \cdot 350\text{ K}}{3.00\text{ L}} = \mathbf{19.15\text{ atm}}\)

Step b: Van der Waals Equation (Repulsive & Attractive Terms)

Calculate both terms separately to avoid calculator bracket errors:

\(p_{\text{vdW}} = \frac{nRT}{V - nb} - a\frac{n^2}{V^2} = \frac{2.00 \cdot 0.08205 \cdot 350}{3.00 - 2.00 \cdot 0.0542} - 6.260 \cdot \frac{2.00^2}{3.00^2}\)
\(p_{\text{vdW}} = \frac{57.435}{2.8916}\text{ atm} - 6.260 \cdot \frac{4.00}{9.00}\text{ atm} = 19.86\text{ atm} - 2.78\text{ atm} = \mathbf{17.08\text{ atm}}\)

Step c: Compression Factor \(Z\) & Interpretation

\(Z = \frac{p_{\text{real}}}{p_{\text{id}}} = \frac{17.08\text{ atm}}{19.15\text{ atm}} = \mathbf{0.892}\)

Interpretation: Since \(Z < 1\) (i.e. \(p_{\text{real}} < p_{\text{id}}\)), attractive forces between the \(\text{Cl}_2\) molecules predominate over repulsive forces under these conditions.

Modeling Example B • First Law of Thermodynamics

Expansion Work of a Gas: Irreversible vs. Isothermal Reversible Process

Ref: L02 Slides 8–15 • In-Slide Ex. 3 • Try-Test 1 Q6
Problem: A cylinder with a piston contains \(n = 1.50\text{ mol}\) of Argon (\(\text{Ar}\), ideal gas) at a temperature of \(\vartheta = 47\text{ }^\circ\text{C}\) (\(T = 320.15\text{ K}\)). The gas expands from an initial volume of \(V_i = 5.00\text{ L}\) to a final volume of \(V_f = 15.0\text{ L}\).
a) Calculate the work \(w_{\text{irrev}}\) done in an irreversible expansion against a constant external pressure of \(p_{\text{ex}} = 1.10\text{ bar}\).
b) Calculate the work \(w_{\text{rev}}\) and heat \(q_{\text{rev}}\) if the expansion occurs isothermally and reversibly.
🎙️ Spoken Board Moderation (English Phrases):

• "In Physical Chemistry we always use the egoistic system convention: When the gas expands (\(\Delta V > 0\)), the system does work on the surroundings and loses energy, so \(w\) MUST be negative."

• "Watch out for the number one unit trap in Step a: Multiplying \(\text{bar}\) by \(\text{Liters}\) does NOT give Joules! Since \(1\text{ bar} = 10^5\text{ Pa}\) and \(1\text{ L} = 10^{-3}\text{ m}^3\), \(1\text{ L}\cdot\text{bar}\) equals exactly \(100\text{ Joules}\)."

• "In Step b, for an isothermal process of an ideal gas, internal energy \(\Delta U = 0\). By the First Law (\(\Delta U = q + w = 0\)), the heat \(q_{\text{rev}}\) entering the cylinder equals \(-w_{\text{rev}}\)."

Step a: Irreversible Expansion (\(\text{bar}\cdot\text{L} \to \text{J}\) Conversion)

Convert to SI units (\(p_{\text{ex}} = 1.10 \cdot 10^5\text{ Pa}\), \(\Delta V = 10.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\text{ m}^3\)) or use \(1\text{ L}\cdot\text{bar} = 100\text{ J}\):

\(w_{\text{irrev}} = -p_{\text{ex}} \cdot (V_f - V_i) = -1.10\text{ bar} \cdot (15.0 - 5.00)\text{ L} = -11.0\text{ L}\cdot\text{bar} = \mathbf{-1100\text{ J} = -1.10\text{ kJ}}\)

Step b: Isothermal Reversible Expansion (\(T = 47 + 273.15 = 320.15\text{ K}\))

\(w_{\text{rev}} = -n \cdot R \cdot T \cdot \ln\left(\frac{V_f}{V_i}\right) = -1.50\text{ mol} \cdot 8.314\text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1} \cdot 320.15\text{ K} \cdot \ln\left(\frac{15.0}{5.00}\right)\)
\(w_{\text{rev}} = -3992.6\text{ J} \cdot 1.0986 = \mathbf{-4386\text{ J} = -4.39\text{ kJ}}\)

Step c: First Law for Isothermal Ideal Gas (\(\Delta U = 0\))

\(\Delta U = q_{\text{rev}} + w_{\text{rev}} = 0 \implies q_{\text{rev}} = -w_{\text{rev}} = \mathbf{+4.39\text{ kJ}}\)

Key Takeaway: The reversible process performs roughly four times as much work (\(|w_{\text{rev}}| > |w_{\text{irrev}}|\)) because the external pressure is continuously matched to the internal gas pressure.
