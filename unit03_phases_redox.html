⚠️ Key Unit & Stoichiometry Traps in Unit 03:
• Clausius-Clapeyron: Convert \(\Delta_{\text{vap}}H\) from \(\text{kJ mol}^{-1}\) to \(\text{J mol}^{-1}\) before dividing by \(R = 8.314\text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}\)! Both pressures \(p_1, p_2\) can be in any unit (\(\text{mbar}\), \(\text{Torr}\), \(\text{bar}\)) as long as both use the same unit.
• Cryoscopy: Molality \(b = \frac{n_{\text{solute}}}{m_{\text{solvent}}(\text{in kg})}\) refers to kilograms of solvent (not grams!).
• Nernst Equation: Always raise species concentrations to their stoichiometric powers (e.g., \([\text{H}^+]^{14}\) or \([\text{Cr}^{3+}]^2\))!

Phases I & II (L05 Slide 24 & L06 Slide 24)

  • Gibbs Phase Rule: \(F = C - P + 2\)  (Pure substance: \(F = 3 - P\))
  • Clausius-Clapeyron Equation:
    \(\ln\left(\frac{p_2}{p_1}\right) = \frac{\Delta_{\text{vap}}H}{R} \cdot \left(\frac{1}{T_1} - \frac{1}{T_2}\right)\)
  • Rearranged for Boiling Temperature \(T_2\):
    \(\frac{1}{T_2} = \frac{1}{T_1} - \frac{R}{\Delta_{\text{vap}}H}\ln\left(\frac{p_2}{p_1}\right) = \frac{1}{T_1} + \frac{R}{\Delta_{\text{vap}}H}\ln\left(\frac{p_1}{p_2}\right)\)
  • Cryoscopy (Freezing Point Depression):
    \(\Delta T_f = |K_f| \cdot b_{\text{solute}} = |K_f| \cdot \frac{m_{\text{solute}}}{M_{\text{solute}} \cdot m_{\text{solvent}}(\text{kg})}\)

Electrochemistry I (L07 Slide 28)

  • Constants: \(F = 96485\text{ C mol}^{-1}\),  \(R = 8.314\text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}\)
  • Gibbs Energy & Cell Voltage: \(\Delta_r G^\circ = -z \cdot F \cdot \Delta E^\circ\)
  • Luther's Rule (Latimer Diagrams):
    \(E^\circ_{\text{total}} = \frac{\sum z_i \cdot E^\circ_i}{\sum z_i} = \frac{z_1 E^\circ_1 + z_2 E^\circ_2}{z_1 + z_2}\)
  • Nernst Equation (Half-Cell \(\text{Ox} + z\,e^- \rightleftharpoons \text{Red}\)):
    \(E = E^\circ + \frac{R \cdot T}{z \cdot F}\ln\left(\frac{a_{\text{Ox}}^{\nu_{\text{Ox}}}}{a_{\text{Red}}^{\nu_{\text{Red}}}}\right) \underset{298\text{ K}}{\approx} E^\circ + \frac{0.05916}{z}\log_{10}\left(\frac{c_{\text{Ox}}^{\nu_{\text{Ox}}}}{c_{\text{Red}}^{\nu_{\text{Red}}}}\right)\)

Modeling Example A • Phases (Clausius-Clapeyron Vacuum Distillation)

Vacuum Distillation of Acetophenone: Required Pressure for Gentle Boiling

Ref: Quiz 1 (2nd Rep, Q4) • L05 Slides 18–22
Problem: The normal boiling point of acetophenone at \(p_1 = 760\text{ Torr}\) (\(1013\text{ mbar}\)) is \(\vartheta_1 = 202.0\text{ }^\circ\text{C}\). Its molar enthalpy of vaporization is \(\Delta_{\text{vap}}H = 43.6\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}\).
To prevent thermal decomposition, we want to distil acetophenone at \(\vartheta_2 = 80.0\text{ }^\circ\text{C}\) in a vacuum distillation apparatus.
a) Convert both temperatures into Kelvin (\(T_1, T_2\)).
b) Calculate the dimensionless right-hand side \(\frac{\Delta_{\text{vap}}H}{R}\left(\frac{1}{T_1} - \frac{1}{T_2}\right)\).
c) Calculate the required pressure \(p_2\) in the distillation apparatus (in \(\text{Torr}\) and \(\text{mbar}\)).
🎙️ Spoken Board Moderation (English Phrases):

• "Why do we use vacuum distillation in organic chemistry? Because high-boiling compounds decompose near \(200\text{ }^\circ\text{C}\), and the Clausius-Clapeyron equation tells us how much we must lower the pressure."

• "First, convert both temperatures to Kelvin! Never plug degrees Celsius into \(1/T_1 - 1/T_2\)."

• "Second, watch the units in \(\Delta_{\text{vap}}H / R\): \(\Delta_{\text{vap}}H\) is \(43600\text{ J mol}^{-1}\), divided by \(8.314\text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}\). Since \(T_2 < T_1\), the bracket \((1/T_1 - 1/T_2)\) is negative, which gives \(p_2 < p_1\)."

Step a: Convert Temperatures to Kelvin

\(T_1 = 202.0 + 273.15 = \mathbf{475.15\text{ K}}\)  |  \(T_2 = 80.0 + 273.15 = \mathbf{353.15\text{ K}}\)

Step b: Evaluate \(\ln(p_2/p_1)\) with \(\Delta_{\text{vap}}H = 43600\text{ J mol}^{-1}\)

\(\ln\left(\frac{p_2}{p_1}\right) = \frac{43600\text{ J mol}^{-1}}{8.314\text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}} \cdot \left(\frac{1}{475.15\text{ K}} - \frac{1}{353.15\text{ K}}\right)\)
\(\ln\left(\frac{p_2}{p_1}\right) = 5244.2\text{ K} \cdot \left(0.0021046 - 0.0028317\right)\text{ K}^{-1} = \mathbf{-3.813}\)

Step c: Solve for \(p_2 = p_1 \cdot \exp(-3.813)\)

\(p_2 = 760\text{ Torr} \cdot e^{-3.813} = 760\text{ Torr} \cdot 0.02208 = \mathbf{16.8\text{ Torr}}\)  \(\left(\text{or } 1013\text{ mbar} \cdot 0.02208 = \mathbf{22.4\text{ mbar}}\right)\)

Modeling Example B • Electrochemistry I (Luther's Rule & Nernst Equation)

Manganese Latimer Diagram & pH-Dependent Reduction Potential

Ref: L07 Slides 17, 26 • Try-Test 1 Q11
Problem: In acidic solution (\(\text{pH} = 0\)), the Latimer diagram for Manganese shows the following standard potentials:
$$\text{MnO}_4^-\text{ ( VII )} \xrightarrow{+1.695\text{ V}} \text{MnO}_2\text{ ( IV )} \xrightarrow{+1.230\text{ V}} \text{Mn}^{2+}\text{ ( II )}$$ a) Determine the electron numbers \(z_1, z_2\) for both steps and calculate the overall standard potential \(E^\circ(\text{MnO}_4^-/\text{Mn}^{2+})\) using Luther's rule.
b) Write the balanced half-cell equation for \(\text{MnO}_4^- \to \text{Mn}^{2+}\) in acidic solution.
c) Calculate the half-cell potential \(E(\text{MnO}_4^-/\text{Mn}^{2+})\) at \(298.15\text{ K}\) if \(\text{pH} = 3.00\) and all other species are at standard activity (\(1.00\text{ M}\)).
🎙️ Spoken Board Moderation (English Phrases):

• "Why are we NOT allowed to simply add two redox potentials \(E^\circ_1 + E^\circ_2\) when combining half-reactions? Because cell voltage is an intensive quantity, whereas Gibbs energy \(\Delta G^\circ = -zFE^\circ\) is extensive!"

• "That leads directly to Luther's rule: We weight each potential \(E^\circ_i\) by the number of transferred electrons \(z_i\) and divide by the total number of electrons \(z_{\text{tot}} = 3 + 2 = 5\)."

• "In Step c, notice that \(8\text{ protons}\) appear on the oxidized side of the half-reaction. At \(\text{pH} = 3\), \([\text{H}^+] = 10^{-3}\text{ M}\), so inside the logarithm we have \((10^{-3})^8 = 10^{-24}\)!"

Step a: Electron Numbers & Luther's Rule

Step 1 (\(\text{+VII} \to \text{+IV}\)): \(z_1 = 3\). Step 2 (\(\text{+IV} \to \text{+II}\)): \(z_2 = 2\). Total electrons: \(z_{\text{tot}} = 3 + 2 = 5\):

\(E^\circ(\text{MnO}_4^-/\text{Mn}^{2+}) = \frac{z_1 \cdot E^\circ_1 + z_2 \cdot E^\circ_2}{z_1 + z_2} = \frac{3 \cdot 1.695\text{ V} + 2 \cdot 1.230\text{ V}}{5} = \frac{7.545\text{ V}}{5} = \mathbf{+1.509\text{ V}}\)

Step b: Balanced Half-Reaction in Acidic Medium

\(\text{MnO}_4^-(aq) + 8\,\text{H}^+(aq) + 5\,e^- \rightleftharpoons \text{Mn}^{2+}(aq) + 4\,\text{H}_2\text{O}(l)\)

Step c: Nernst Equation at \(\text{pH} = 3.00 \implies [\text{H}^+] = 10^{-3}\text{ M}\)

\(E = E^\circ + \frac{0.05916\text{ V}}{5} \cdot \log_{10}\left(\frac{[\text{MnO}_4^-] \cdot [\text{H}^+]^8}{[\text{Mn}^{2+}]}\right) = 1.509\text{ V} + \frac{0.05916}{5} \cdot \log_{10}\left((10^{-3})^8\right)\)
\(E = 1.509\text{ V} + 0.01183\text{ V} \cdot (-24) = 1.509\text{ V} - 0.284\text{ V} = \mathbf{+1.225\text{ V}}\)

Insight: Raising the pH from \(0\) to \(3\) lowers the oxidizing power of permanganate by nearly \(300\text{ mV}\) because \(8\,\text{H}^+\) ions are consumed per \(\text{MnO}_4^-\)!
