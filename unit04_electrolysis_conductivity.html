🧠 The 5 Fundamental "By Heart" Formulas (Required without Formula Sheet in Exams!)
$$\rho = \frac{m}{V}$$
$$n = \frac{m}{M}$$
$$c = \frac{n}{V}$$
$$p \cdot V = n \cdot R \cdot T$$
$$x_i = \frac{n_i}{n_{\text{tot}}}$$

1. Decomposition Voltage & Faraday's Laws

Theoretical Decomposition Voltage & Terminal Voltage:

$$E_{\text{decomp}}^\circ = E_{\text{anode}}^\circ - E_{\text{cathode}}^\circ = |E_{\text{cell}}^\circ|$$ $$U_{\text{applied}} = E_{\text{decomp}}^\circ + \eta_{\text{anode}} + |\eta_{\text{cathode}}| + I \cdot R_{\text{cell}}$$

Faraday's First & Second Laws (including Current Efficiency $\eta$):

$$Q = I \cdot t = n_e \cdot F = z \cdot n_{\text{theoretical}} \cdot F$$ $$m_{\text{actual}} = \eta \cdot \frac{I \cdot t \cdot M}{z \cdot F} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \eta = \frac{m_{\text{actual}}}{m_{\text{theoretical}}}$$

Electrical Energy Consumption: $W_{\text{el}} = U_{\text{applied}} \cdot Q = U_{\text{applied}} \cdot I \cdot t$ (in $\text{J} = \text{V A s}$)

2. Electrolytic Conductivity & Ostwald's Law

Conductance $G$, Cell Constant $K_{\text{cell}}$ & Specific Conductivity $\kappa$:

$$G = \frac{1}{R} = \kappa \cdot \frac{A}{l} = \frac{\kappa}{K_{\text{cell}}} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \kappa = \frac{K_{\text{cell}}}{R} \quad \left(K_{\text{cell}} = \frac{l}{A}\right)$$

Molar ($\Lambda_m$) & Equivalent Conductivity ($\Lambda_{\text{eq}}$):

$$\Lambda_m = \frac{\kappa}{c}, \qquad \Lambda_{\text{eq}} = \frac{\kappa}{c_{\text{eq}}} = \frac{\Lambda_m}{z^*} \quad (z^* = \nu_+ |z_+| = \nu_- |z_-|)$$

Kohlrausch's Law & Ostwald's Dilution Law (Weak Electrolytes):

$$\Lambda_m^\circ = \nu_+ \lambda_+^\circ + \nu_- \lambda_-^\circ, \qquad \alpha = \frac{\Lambda_m}{\Lambda_m^\circ}, \qquad K_c = \frac{\alpha^2 \cdot c}{1 - \alpha}$$
Constants & Molar Masses:
  • Faraday constant: $F = 96485\text{ C mol}^{-1} = 96485\text{ A s mol}^{-1}$
  • Molar masses: $M(\text{Al}) = 26.98\text{ g mol}^{-1}$, $M(\text{Ag}) = 107.87\text{ g mol}^{-1}$, $M(\text{Cu}) = 63.55\text{ g mol}^{-1}$
  • Standard potentials ($25\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$, $\text{pH } 0$): $E^\circ(\text{O}_2/\text{H}_2\text{O}) = +1.229\text{ V}$, $E^\circ(\text{Cl}_2/\text{Cl}^-) = +1.358\text{ V}$
⚠️ Golden Unit Conversion Rules for Conductivity:
  • Lab formula: $\Lambda_m\text{ [S cm}^2\text{ mol}^{-1}\text{]} = \dfrac{\kappa\text{ [S cm}^{-1}\text{]} \cdot 1000}{c\text{ [mol L}^{-1}\text{]}} = \dfrac{\kappa\text{ [mS cm}^{-1}\text{]}}{c\text{ [mol L}^{-1}\text{]}}$
  • SI conversion: $1\text{ S cm}^2\text{ mol}^{-1} = 10^{-4}\text{ S m}^2\text{ mol}^{-1} = 0.1\text{ mS m}^2\text{ mol}^{-1}$
  • Concentration: $1\text{ mol L}^{-1} = 10^{-3}\text{ mol cm}^{-3} = 1000\text{ mol m}^{-3}$

Guided Example 1 • Ref: 08_Electrochemistry II.pdf & Q01 (3rd Rep, Q1)

Industrial Hall-Héroult Melt Electrolysis of $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ ($1\text{ t}$ Aluminum & Current Efficiency $\eta$)

Problem Statement: Metallic aluminum ($M = 26.98\text{ g mol}^{-1}$) is produced industrially via the Hall-Héroult process by electrolyzing $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ dissolved in molten cryolite ($\text{Na}_3\text{AlF}_6$) at $\vartheta \approx 960\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$. An industrial electrolysis pot operates at a current efficiency of $\eta = 88.0\%$ over a $t = 24.0\text{ h}$ production shift to produce exactly $m_{\text{Al}} = 1.00\text{ metric ton}$ ($1.00 \times 10^6\text{ g}$) of aluminum at a cell voltage of $U = 4.20\text{ V}$.
(a) Write the cathode reduction half-reaction, identify the stoichiometric electron number $z$, and calculate the theoretical charge $Q_{\text{theo}}$ (in $\text{C}$).
(b) Account for the current efficiency $\eta = 88.0\%$ to determine the actual charge $Q_{\text{actual}}$ and the required continuous operating current $I$ (in $\text{kA}$).
(c) Calculate the specific electrical energy consumption in $\text{kWh}$ per $\text{kg}$ of produced aluminum ($1\text{ kWh} = 3.60 \times 10^6\text{ J}$).

🎙️ Lecturer Board Moderation (Guided Example 1):

"First, ask students why we cannot electrolyze aqueous $\text{AlCl}_3$ to get aluminum metal: in water, $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ is reduced to $\text{H}_2$ long before $\text{Al}^{3+}$ ($E^\circ = -1.66\text{ V}$). Hence we need molten cryolite!"

"Second, highlight the classic exam trap with current efficiency $\eta$: If only $88\%$ of the electrons reduce $\text{Al}^{3+}$, do we need MORE or LESS total current from the power grid to produce $1\text{ ton}$ of Al? We need MORE current, so we divide $Q_{\text{theo}}$ by $\eta = 0.88$!"

Step (a) – Stoichiometry & Theoretical Charge $Q_{\text{theo}}$:

Cathode reduction: $\text{Al}^{3+} + 3e^- \longrightarrow \text{Al(l)} \implies z = 3$ moles of electrons per mole of $\text{Al}$.

Amount of aluminum for $m_{\text{Al}} = 1.00 \times 10^6\text{ g}$:

$$n_{\text{Al}} = \frac{m_{\text{Al}}}{M_{\text{Al}}} = \frac{1.00 \times 10^6\text{ g}}{26.98\text{ g mol}^{-1}} = 3.7064 \times 10^4\text{ mol}$$ $$Q_{\text{theo}} = z \cdot n_{\text{Al}} \cdot F = 3 \cdot 3.7064 \times 10^4\text{ mol} \cdot 96485\text{ C mol}^{-1} = \mathbf{1.0728 \times 10^{10}\text{ C}}$$
Step (b) – Actual Charge $Q_{\text{actual}}$ & Operating Current $I$:

Since $\eta = 0.880$, more total charge must pass through the cell ($t = 24.0\text{ h} = 86400\text{ s}$):

$$Q_{\text{actual}} = \frac{Q_{\text{theo}}}{\eta} = \frac{1.0728 \times 10^{10}\text{ C}}{0.880} = \mathbf{1.2191 \times 10^{10}\text{ C}}$$ $$I = \frac{Q_{\text{actual}}}{t} = \frac{1.2191 \times 10^{10}\text{ A s}}{86400\text{ s}} = 1.4110 \times 10^5\text{ A} = \mathbf{141.1\text{ kA}}$$
Step (c) – Specific Electrical Energy Consumption:

Total electrical work $W_{\text{el}}$ at $U = 4.20\text{ V}$ for $1000\text{ kg}$ of $\text{Al}$:

$$W_{\text{el}} = U \cdot Q_{\text{actual}} = 4.20\text{ V} \cdot 1.2191 \times 10^{10}\text{ C} = 5.120 \times 10^{10}\text{ J}$$ $$W_{\text{el}}\text{ [kWh]} = \frac{5.120 \times 10^{10}\text{ J}}{3.60 \times 10^6\text{ J kWh}^{-1}} = 14223\text{ kWh} \implies w_{\text{spec}} = \frac{14223\text{ kWh}}{1000\text{ kg}} = \mathbf{14.22\text{ kWh kg}^{-1}}$$
Guided Example 2 • Ref: 09_Electrochemistry III.pdf, Q01 (3rd Rep, Q2) & T01 (Q12)

Electrolytic Conductivity ($\kappa, \Lambda_m$) & Ostwald's Dilution Law for Acetic Acid

Problem Statement: A conductivity measurement cell with electrode spacing $l = 1.20\text{ cm}$ and effective area $A = 1.50\text{ cm}^2$ is filled with an aqueous acetic acid ($\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$) solution of concentration $c = 0.0500\text{ mol L}^{-1}$ at $\vartheta = 25.0\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$. The measured resistance is $R = 2210\text{ }\Omega$. At $25.0\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$, the limiting molar ionic conductivities are $\lambda^\circ(\text{H}^+) = 349.8\text{ S cm}^2\text{ mol}^{-1}$ and $\lambda^\circ(\text{CH}_3\text{COO}^-) = 40.9\text{ S cm}^2\text{ mol}^{-1}$.
(a) Calculate the cell constant $K_{\text{cell}}$ (in $\text{cm}^{-1}$) and the specific conductivity $\kappa$ (in $\mu\text{S cm}^{-1}$ and $\text{S cm}^{-1}$).
(b) Determine the molar conductivity $\Lambda_m$ both in classical laboratory units ($\text{S cm}^2\text{ mol}^{-1}$) and in strict SI units ($\text{S m}^2\text{ mol}^{-1}$).
(c) Calculate the limiting molar conductivity $\Lambda_m^\circ$, the degree of dissociation $\alpha$, and the acid dissociation constant $K_a$ via Ostwald's Dilution Law.

🎙️ Lecturer Board Moderation (Guided Example 2):

"Point out why $\Lambda_m = \kappa / c$ is the #1 unit trap in Physical Chemistry exams: $c$ is given in $\text{mol L}^{-1} = \text{mol dm}^{-3}$, whereas $\kappa$ uses $\text{cm}^{-1}$. Since $1\text{ L} = 1000\text{ cm}^3$, we MUST write $c\text{ [mol cm}^{-3}\text{]} = c\text{ [mol L}^{-1}\text{]} / 1000$."

"Then show the shortcut: if $\kappa$ is converted to $\text{mS cm}^{-1}$, the factor $1000$ cancels out directly: $\Lambda_m\text{ [S cm}^2\text{ mol}^{-1}\text{]} = \kappa\text{ [mS cm}^{-1}\text{]} / c\text{ [mol L}^{-1}\text{]}$!"

Step (a) – Cell Constant $K_{\text{cell}}$ & Specific Conductivity $\kappa$:
$$K_{\text{cell}} = \frac{l}{A} = \frac{1.20\text{ cm}}{1.50\text{ cm}^2} = \mathbf{0.800\text{ cm}^{-1}}$$ $$\kappa = \frac{K_{\text{cell}}}{R} = \frac{0.800\text{ cm}^{-1}}{2210\text{ }\Omega} = \mathbf{3.620 \times 10^{-4}\text{ S cm}^{-1}} = \mathbf{362.0\text{ }\mu\text{S cm}^{-1}} = 0.3620\text{ mS cm}^{-1}$$
Step (b) – Molar Conductivity $\Lambda_m$ in Lab & SI Units:

With $c = 0.0500\text{ mol L}^{-1} = 5.00 \times 10^{-5}\text{ mol cm}^{-3}$:

$$\Lambda_m = \frac{\kappa}{c} = \frac{3.620 \times 10^{-4}\text{ S cm}^{-1}}{5.00 \times 10^{-5}\text{ mol cm}^{-3}} = \mathbf{7.240\text{ S cm}^2\text{ mol}^{-1}}$$

Converting to SI ($\times 10^{-4}$ since $1\text{ cm}^2 = 10^{-4}\text{ m}^2$):

$$\Lambda_m\text{ (SI)} = 7.240 \times 10^{-4}\text{ S m}^2\text{ mol}^{-1} = \mathbf{0.7240\text{ mS m}^2\text{ mol}^{-1}}$$
Step (c) – Kohlrausch's Law, Dissociation Degree $\alpha$ & Ostwald's Constant $K_a$:
$$\Lambda_m^\circ = \lambda^\circ(\text{H}^+) + \lambda^\circ(\text{CH}_3\text{COO}^-) = 349.8 + 40.9 = \mathbf{390.7\text{ S cm}^2\text{ mol}^{-1}}$$ $$\alpha = \frac{\Lambda_m}{\Lambda_m^\circ} = \frac{7.240}{390.7} = \mathbf{0.01853} \quad (\mathbf{1.853\%})$$ $$K_a = \frac{\alpha^2 \cdot c}{1 - \alpha} = \frac{(0.01853)^2 \cdot 0.0500\text{ mol L}^{-1}}{1 - 0.01853} = \mathbf{1.749 \times 10^{-5}\text{ mol L}^{-1}}$$
